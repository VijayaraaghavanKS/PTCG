## In progress (2026-08-11, post session-reset)

Motivated by two things: (a) Alakazam needs a replacement, (b) the competition's own description explicitly states pure rule-based play is insufficient ("Winning a Pokémon TCG game requires forward thinking, real-time adaptation, and optimal decision-making... must demonstrate high analytical capacity, adaptability, and be ready for the unexpected") — validating a push toward hybrid rule-based + search + learned components, not just another hand-tuned heuristic deck.

Real-world competitive TCG research (see below) surfaced a pattern we hadn't used yet: **favor 1-Prize attackers** for prize-trade efficiency — every deck we've built or tested so far (Dragapult ex, Bellibolt ex, Mega Lucario ex, Alakazam, Mist Energy) is a multi-Prize (`ex`/Mega) attacker deck.

### Jolteon ex (jolteon_v1) — Stage 1, burst damage (COMPLETE — rejected)
- **Why**: 2nd-highest Utility score of any card in the entire ~1267-card pool, per our card-value scoring formula. Stage 1 only (Eevee → Jolteon ex, no Stage 2) — deliberately chosen to avoid the exact slow-setup failure mode that killed Alakazam and Mist Energy. Multi-Prize (concedes 2 on KO) — a real tradeoff against the 1-Prize-attacker principle above, accepted here in exchange for speed.
- **Deck**: 4 Eevee / 4 Jolteon ex, 5 fragile "ammo tank" support Pokémon (Misty's Magikarp x4, Poltchageist x1 — damage-immune while benched, fed to Flashing Spear's bench-energy-discard bonus), 35 Trainers, 12 Energy (8 Lightning, 2 Fighting, 2 Water for the Dravite tech attack). Validated legal.
- **Bugs found and fixed**: (1) energy-attach starvation — attaching combat energy to the active attacker scored far below routine (and unlimited-per-turn) trainer plays, so the agent essentially never attached energy at all (confirmed via trace: zero attacks landed across full games) — fixed by raising attach-to-attacker priority above the trainer band. (2) An initial lethal-override fix overcorrected into near-total passivity (1 attack in 11 turns) by declining *any* non-KO attack whenever "exposed" — narrowed to only decline genuinely bad-trade KOs.
- **Final local win rates (16 games each)**: **0/16 (0%) vs dragapult_day1, 1/16 (6%) vs mega_lucario_ref, 0/16 (0%) vs iono_day1**. Blended ≈2%. Confirmed via direct exception-tracing that there are zero crashes/silent fallbacks — losses are real strategic outcomes, not robustness bugs.
- **Root cause**: the deck's core mechanic (build bench energy over several turns, then burst for up to 240 via Flashing Spear) still needs multi-turn setup time, and our band's meta decks (200-320 HP, 100+ effective DPE) don't grant it — traced games show only 1-2 attacks landing before losing the whole board. The 5 fragile 30 HP "ammo tanks" feed cheap prizes without buying real tempo.
- **Status**: **Rejected — not submission-ready.**
- **Reframes the earlier "Stage 2 is the problem" hypothesis**: Jolteon ex is Stage 1 and still lost just as badly as the Stage-2 decks. The real common thread across all 5 new archetypes tried (Alakazam, Mist Energy, Greninja ex, Inteleon, Jolteon ex) is **any deck needing multiple turns of ramp/combo setup — evolution-based or energy-based — loses to our band's fast, high-raw-HP/damage meta**, not evolution stage specifically.

### Inteleon (inteleon_v1) — Stage 2, 1-Prize attacker (COMPLETE — rejected)
- **Why**: Highest Utility of any non-`ex`/non-Mega card in the pool, and a genuine 1-Prize attacker matching real-world competitive theory's prize-trade-efficiency principle. Stage 2 (Sobble→Drizzile→Inteleon) is a real speed risk given our last two failures — deck was deliberately built with the consistency package maxed out (4x each of Rare Candy, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Ultra Ball, Poké Pad, Dawn, Hilda, Night Stretcher, Boss's Orders, plus 1x Precious Trolley ACE SPEC) to mitigate.

Zero crashes/bugs found — full move-by-move trace confirmed clean engine-sourced damage calculations and correct decision logic throughout.

Sobble (70 HP) / Drizzile (100 HP) get one-shot by the meta's 130-270 damage attacks before Inteleon (150 HP) ever comes online; by the time it does, the Prize race is already unrecoverable (traced games show 5-6 straight opponent turns with zero KOs credited to us while their prize count ticks down). The 1-Prize-attacker efficiency principle from real-world TCG theory is real, but it's moot if the deck can't survive long enough to use it.
- **Status**: **Rejected — not submission-ready.** This is the 3rd Stage-2 archetype to fail for essentially the same structural reason (see Alakazam, Mist Energy, and Greninja ex below) — strong, repeated evidence that **Stage 2 setups cannot survive our rating band's aggressive starter-kit meta**, independent of how much consistency tech is thrown at them. This is a load-bearing finding for the eventual Strategy Category write-up.

### Greninja ex (greninja_v1) — Stage 2, highest raw DPE (COMPLETE — rejected)
- **Why**: Highest damage-per-energy (170) of any card in the entire pool. Highest-risk combination of our 3 new concepts: Stage 2 **and** multi-Prize.
- **Deck**: 4 Froakie / 4 Frogadier / 4 Greninja ex, 36 Trainers (Rare Candy/Buddy-Buddy Poffin/Ultra Ball/Dawn/Boss's Orders/Night Stretcher x4 each, Pokégear 3.0/Hilda x3, Carmine/Switch/Lillie's Determination x2), 12 Basic Water Energy. Validated legal.

(2) Loss-shielding retreat scores (7k-12k) were completely dwarfed by routine attack scores (100k+), so the "protect the 2-prize Greninja ex" logic could never mathematically fire — rescaled to 150k (below lethal-attack priority, above routine-attack priority).
- **Final local win rates (16 games each)**: **6/16 (37.5%) vs mega_lucario_ref, 5/16 (31.25%) vs iono_day1, 1/16 (6.25%) vs dragapult_day1**. Blended ≈25%. Zero crashes.

### ml_v1 — RL/DL exploration (COMPLETE — rejected)
- **Why**: Direct response to the competition's stated need for adaptability beyond pure rule-based play, and to test whether a learned component (imitation learning from real replay data) can beat hand-tuned heuristics, matching the "novel algorithm" push.
- **Approach**: Confirmed the replay schema (`research/episodes/*/*.json` → `steps[i][playerSlot]` holds an `observation` matching `cg.api.Observation`'s shape exactly, plus the `action` taken — genuine move-level (state, action) pairs, offset by one: `steps[i][p].action` answers the decision posed in `steps[i-1][p].observation.select`). This made supervised imitation learning genuinely viable, not just self-play weight-tuning. Built a deck-agnostic 130-dim feature extractor (select-type/context, option-type, area, referenced card's generic stats — never raw card IDs, since replay decks differ from ours) and trained a plain logistic regression offline (scikit-learn) on 8,639 real decision events extracted from all 60 episode files. Found and fixed a real bug in the extractor itself (an else-branch appending 6 zeros instead of 7, silently dropping RETREAT/END/YES-NO/ATTACK/NUMBER rows via a swallowed exception). Deployment has zero runtime dependencies (pure stdlib dot-product against exported `weights.json`) to avoid any risk of missing packages in the Kaggle sandbox.
- **Offline eval**: 53.3% top-1 validation accuracy vs. 37.4% for "always pick the first option" baseline — a real but modest signal.
- **Live local testing (16 games each, using mega_lucario_ref's deck so the matchup isolates policy quality)**: **1/16 (6%) vs dragapult_day1, 1/16 (6%) vs iono_day1, 5/16 (31%) vs mega_lucario_ref (same deck, mirror match)**. No crashes/illegal moves across any game — mechanically sound.
- **Root cause of underperformance**: a single pointwise linear model over generic features can't capture the multi-turn tactical planning (attack-combo lookahead, KO sequencing) that the hand-tuned heuristics explicitly encode. Losing 69% of a mirror match against the heuristic on the *identical* deck is a clean, unambiguous signal — this isn't a deck problem, it's a policy-quality gap.
- **Status**: **Rejected — not a submission candidate.** Honest verdict from the build: not worth further investment this week; if pursued later, the credible next step is using this as a reranker layered on top of an existing heuristic (not a replacement), which is more work than is justified before the deadline. Pure heuristic refinement is the better use of remaining time. Files at `agent/ml_v1/` kept for reference.

### ga_v1 — genetic-algorithm weight tuning (COMPLETE — inconclusive/rejected)
- **Why**: Direct response to the user asking why we hadn't tried more sophisticated optimization (AlphaZero/minimax/GA/ensemble were all raised). AlphaZero ruled out as infeasible (see research section below); GA flagged as cheap, CPU-only, low-risk since it tunes existing heuristic coefficients rather than replacing the tactical logic.
- **Approach**: Reused `mega_lucario_ref/deck.csv` unmodified (isolates weight-tuning method from deck novelty). Built a clean-room staged-policy agent with 16 tunable weights (damage progress, KO prize value, energy attachment priorities, evolution progress, card draw, bench development, retreat danger, etc.) loaded from an external JSON. GA: population 24, tournament selection, arithmetic crossover, gaussian mutation, elitism 2. Engine is extremely cheap to run (~23ms/game via the native lib), enabling 12,096 self-play games in 269s for the final run.
- **Key finding (methodological)**: a first pass (9 games/candidate fitness, 40 generations) crowned a "best" individual that then scored *worse* than the hand-guessed baseline in a proper 48-game eval (29.2% vs 43.8%) — classic overfitting to small-N fitness noise. Fixed by doubling games/candidate and adding a 72-game low-noise validation pass over the hall-of-fame before picking a final winner.
- **Final 48-game evaluation** (16 games each vs dragapult_day1/iono_day1/mega_lucario_ref): **GA-tuned: 21/48 (43.8%)** (5/16, 10/16, 6/16) vs **hand-guessed baseline: 22/48 (45.8%)** (3/16, 12/16, 7/16) — statistically indistinguishable, a 1-game difference.
- **Verdict**: GA did not measurably beat sensible hand-guessing at this compute budget — fitness noise from single-game variance dominates at 18-72 games/candidate; would need meaningfully more games per fitness eval to reliably separate weight vectors with a true skill gap under ~10-15 points (the fast engine could support this — total wall-clock was under 9 minutes — but wasn't pursued further given time remaining). **Not a submission candidate** — neither weight set cracked 50% blended win rate. Recommendation from the build: prioritize getting decks' hand-coded tactical logic (KO detection, loss-shielding) correct first — that's where the real win-rate gap lives, not the numeric coefficients.
- **Notable meta-finding**: even a clean-room agent piloting the exact archetype (Mega Lucario ex) that dominates our real rating band (~29% share) couldn't clear 45% locally against our 3 references. Combined with 5 other failed new-archetype attempts, this suggests our reference decks (two of which are our own real, tuned Day-1 submissions; the third an official sample notebook) are a genuinely high bar for hours-old clean-room builds to clear — not that every new archetype we've picked happens to be individually bad.

## Research: hybrid rule-based + ML/RL feasibility (2026-08-11)

Motivated directly by the competition's own description: *"Using rule-based programming alone may not ensure a high ranking... forward thinking, real-time adaptation, and optimal decision-making... must demonstrate high analytical capacity, adaptability, and be ready for the unexpected."* Full findings below inform both agent design and the eventual Strategy Category report.

**What "real-time adaptation" most plausibly means here**: within-match opponent-archetype belief tracking (a running estimate of what the opponent is playing, updated as cards are revealed, used to bias decisions) — not between-game model learning. The per-match time budget (10 min, ~0.8s decision windows) rules out live weight updates entirely.

**Concrete evidence against full RL/AlphaZero-style approaches, found from inside this actual competition** (via a public GitHub repo, `TomBombadyl/kaggle_pokemon`, reporting real TrueSkill μ ratings): a real RL+MCTS submission scored **580.6μ**, while a heuristic+search-scorer hybrid on the *same deck* scored **660.5μ** — RL genuinely underperformed the simpler approach here, not hypothetically. A visible competitor (rank ~143) experimenting with RL confirms on the forums it's "not contributing to score yet." The nearest real academic analog (the multi-year "Strategy Card Game AI Competition" on Legends of Code and Magic) took *years* before a learned end-to-end agent (ByteRL) finally beat search+heuristic hybrids — and even ByteRL's own behavior-cloning-only baseline, trained on far more data (3M state-action pairs) than we have (~60 replay episodes), plateaued at only ~42% win rate against its RL-finetuned target.

**Full CFR and full information-set-tree ISMCTS are both ruled out** as intractable in the time/engineering budget available — CFR needs abstraction infrastructure we don't have, ISMCTS needs re-architecting the search's tree indexing around an SDK whose tree-sharing capabilities are still unknown. Reassuringly, the "theoretically flawed" determinized/PIMC-style search we're already using is empirically strong in the wider literature (expert-level play in Bridge/Skat/Hearts despite known strategy-fusion issues) — so the existing architecture is a sound foundation, not a dead end.

**Ranked next steps** (all additive/toggleable on top of the existing architecture, not replacements — deliberately chosen given the hard "never crash" reliability requirement):
1. Extend opponent-archetype belief tracking beyond the search gate into the heuristic fallback paths too (highest payoff, lowest risk, ~0.5-1 day).
2. Audit the search's determinization-aggregation for a "strategy fusion" bug (fixed root actions scored across worlds vs. each world independently picking its own best move) — cheap, ~0.5 day, literature-backed fix if present.
3. Small learned evaluator (logistic regression/tiny MLP) swapped into the existing 2-ply search's scoring function, trained on self-play + replay data as a pretraining seed — medium effort, ~1.5-2 days, kept strictly togglable.
4. "ISMCTS-lite" (root-parallelized search, shared stats only at the root) — stretch goal only, attempt last if time remains.
- Explicitly do NOT attempt full ISMCTS or CFR this week.

## v2 upgrades: belief-tracking + fusion-safe search (2026-08-11)

Direct execution of the research report's top recommendations, applied to our 3 best-understood decks rather than another from-scratch archetype. Each v2 keeps its v1's proven staged heuristic fully intact and adds two layers on top: (1) persistent opponent-archetype belief tracking, recomputed every turn from revealed opponent cards, feeding into loss-shielding/retreat/sniping priority and determinization sampling; (2) a fusion-safe shallow search via the engine's Search API — fixes top-K heuristic candidates *before* sampling hidden-info determinizations (avoiding the "strategy fusion" failure mode), evaluates every candidate against every sampled world, averages, only overrides on a large score margin. Both layers are hard-gated to fall back to the plain v1 heuristic on any error/timeout — never risk the "must never crash" requirement.

### Dragapult v2 (dragapult_v2)
- **Design**: search gated to MAIN phase, turn≥2, 3-16 options, 0.35s budget (well under the 0.8s reference used by the public 950+ agent). Fixes top-3 candidates before sampling belief-weighted determinizations, overrides only on ≥450-point margin.
- **A/B vs dragapult_day1 (its own v1)**: **57.5% (23-17) over 40 games**, positive in both independent 20-game batches.
- **vs iono_day1**: 59.4% (19/32) — notably below dragapult_day1's own baseline there (81.25%, 13/16) — a possible archetype-specific regression, not fully disambiguated from noise at this sample size.
- **vs mega_lucario_ref**: 56.25% (9/16).
- **Engineering**: zero search errors/crashes across ~130 games, search fires on ~55% of MAIN decisions, overrides the heuristic ~6-9% of the times it fires, avg 7-8ms/decision (max 15.9ms) — trivially within the 10-min/match budget. Override/outcome correlation check found no measurable signal at this sample size (i.e. the win-rate lift likely comes more from persistent belief-tracking touching every turn than from the occasional search override).
- **Verdict**: real but modest improvement, not a slam dunk. Recommend a larger (~100+ game) validation specifically vs iono_day1 before retiring dragapult_day1 as the live submission.

**Follow-up escalation pass (2026-08-11, same day)**: Ran ~3,500+ games across large-N A/B tests, ablations, and a precision sweep to chase the iono weakness. **Also turned out to be pure sampling noise, not a real regression**: a self-play control (day1 vs itself, which must average 50%) swung from 43.0% at n=300 to 51.6% at n=800 on its own. At n=600+, dragapult_v2 vs iono_day1 sits at ~67%, statistically identical to dragapult_day1's own freshly-measured ~66.4% — **the edge comes from the shared base heuristic/decklist, not from v2's belief/search additions specifically.** Neither bellibolt_v2's bug pattern (over-triggering belief retreat) nor its full-turn-rollout bias signature reproduced here.
- **Escalation tested** (determinizations 3→20, candidates 3→5, override margin 450→150, budget 0.35→0.6s, individually and combined): **none produced a statistically significant change in any matchup** — all within ~3-4 points of baseline, inside the noise floor even at n=150-300.
- **Modest hardening tune kept** (not a proven win-rate fix, just reduces per-decision search noise): determinizations 3→6, override margin 450→350, time budget 0.35s→0.5s.
- **Final large-N results** (n=300 each): vs iono_day1 67.3%, vs dragapult_day1 (A/B) 52.3%, vs mega_lucario_ref 48.7% — essentially a coin-flip against its own v1, no clear edge anywhere.
- **Status: NOT a clear, robust improvement over the live dragapult_day1 (currently hot at 718-735).** Belief+search layer is safe (never measured to lose games net) but hasn't been shown to add real strength here — further gains would need heuristic-level changes, not more search compute. **Recommendation: do not replace the live submission with this.**

### Mega Lucario v2 (mega_lucario_v2)
- **Design**: starting weights = ga_v1's GA-evolved vector. Search gated to MAIN phase, turn≥2, 3-18 options, 0.35s budget; fixes 2-3 candidates before sampling belief-biased determinizations, overrides only on ≥450-point margin.
- **Bug found and fixed**: initial search leaf-evaluator only scored the board right after our own turn, no look-ahead to the opponent's reply — caused search to override *away* from correct retreats into fatal exposure. Fixed by adding a cheap static one-ply threat penalty. Verified via ablation (n=80): before fix, search-enabled configs *lost* to search-disabled; after fix, full v2 hit 67.5% vs ga_v1 and search-only hit 57.5% — both techniques now add real value.
- **Belief tracking**: verified via trace to converge to 100% correct archetype confidence by turn 14 in a live game, never misidentified across testing.
- **A/B vs ga_v1 (its own v1)**: official 20g = 25%, but noise-dominated — confirmatory n=100 = **50/100 (50.0%), parity, not a regression**. **Headline finding — other teams are hitting 800-1034 on the SAME core archetypes we run (Dragapult ex, Bellibolt, Mega Lucario), using techniques we haven't tried:**

Explicitly tried and rejected forward search (value model has good AUC 0.91 on isolated positions but can't rank *sibling* positions within a turn, which is exactly what search needs — direct explanation for why our own search additions never helped). Also found ladder noise is severe: byte-identical resubmits scored 940.7 vs 790.8 in one case, "928→700" in another.
- `cha7ura/kaggle-pokemon-tcg`: doctrine is **counter the current #1 deck, don't mirror it** — mirroring the rising #1 (Trevenant) flopped at 460; shipping its hard counter hit 900-978. A GA-tuned candidate that improved local win-rate +0.057 scored **-86 points live** — direct warning about trusting local A/B deltas, matches our own GA/search null results.
- `yowayani517/pokemon-tcg-ai-battle`: controlled 4-way same-meta comparison — rule-based wall 82%, self-play RL 54%, forward search 53% (worse than the wall), **"plan-then-subordinate" 85%** (best). Technique: at turn start, fully solve for ONE fixed attack goal via exhaustive search over that turn only, then subordinate every other decision this turn (evolve/attach/retreat/trainer plays) to serve that already-committed plan, instead of scoring each decision independently. A structural heuristic-architecture change, not a weight-tuning one.
- `sutesute0000/ptcg-ai-battle-lopunny`: adopted its deck via pure meta-data mining (highest observed win-rate deck, no opponent-modeling). - `henriquetakahiroito/pokemon-tcg-ai-battle`: mined 6,533 real episode JSONs for what top-rated agents' decks actually were, pivoted deck choice based on that mining (not better play) — 600→948 in one hour from a deck swap alone.
- `knightynite/ptcg-ai-battle-agent`: Common Random Numbers (CRN) paired-world A/B testing — run "before"/"after" policy against the *same* sampled hidden-info world/seed, not independent random games, to isolate real policy deltas from world-to-world variance. Worth adopting for our own local testing given how much noise every source reports.

**Synthesis — top actionable levers for our ~265-500 point gap, ranked:**
1. **Behavior-clone from real ladder replay data (win-filtered), using Kaggle's official daily episode-index dataset.** The single most repeated, highest-magnitude signal across 3 independent teams (jibai-devs, rahulsiiitm, Jun-Morita) — all broke a ~600-700 ceiling this exact way, on our exact archetypes. Different from our earlier ml_v1 attempt (small logistic regression, 8,639 unfiltered decisions from our own scattered local episodes, no win-filtering) — the winning pattern is a stronger model (GBDT/random forest), win-filtered examples, and ideally sourced from top-player replays specifically.
2. **Plan-then-subordinate heuristic architecture** (yowayani517: 85% vs 82% heuristic-wall vs 53% forward search) — cheap, no ML needed, could be layered onto dragapult_day1 directly.
3. **Meta-counter targeting via continuous episode mining** — counter the *current* #1 deck rather than inventing novel archetypes (which we already tried 7x and lost).
4. **Distrust local win-rate deltas more** — multiple independent teams report large noise (100-250pt swings on byte-identical resubmits) and offline/local metrics contradicting real ladder strength on repeated occasions. Reinforces our own noise-floor lesson above; adopt N≥120/opponent and CRN-paired testing going forward.
5. **Stadium-aware micro-heuristics + cheap opponent-archetype classifier** (jibai-devs' `opponent_belief`: independent per-archetype log-odds Naive Bayes detectors trained on 313k real decks, stdlib-only, cheap to vendor) as a lower-effort parallel improvement.

## Plan-then-subordinate: dragapult_plan_v1 (2026-08-12, COMPLETE — not adopted)
Parallel follow-up to the same research, implementing `yowayani517`'s public "Planning" architecture (82%→85% win rate in their own controlled test) on top of `dragapult_day1`.

- **Architecture**: at the first MAIN-phase decision each turn, uses `cg.api` search to branch over this turn's own remaining decisions only (never the opponent's reply — a cheaper, different lever than the adversarial search already tried and rejected in dragapult_v2/v3), picks the best-leaf branch, stores it as a persistent turn plan (attacker, attack, evolve/attach targets, trainer-play sequence), and gives small additive bonuses to decisions this turn that progress the plan. Falls back cleanly to plain `dragapult_day1` behavior if the plan becomes infeasible or search is unavailable (verified via a forced-failure smoke test, 15/15 clean).
- **Bug found and fixed**: an initial large bonus for matching the plan's chosen attack was catastrophic (5/30 in ablation) — it broke the base heuristic's implicit "develop board before attacking" priority, which is exactly how Dragapult ex's real win condition works (delay Phantom Dive for a multi-KO turn). Fixed by dropping the attack-type bonus entirely, keeping only evolve/attach/trainer-play sequencing bonuses. Second bug: the rollout's own attack-scoring didn't distinguish Phantom Dive (154) from the much weaker Jet Headbutt (153), sometimes locking a plan onto the wrong attack — fixed by weighting rollout attack scores by real attack damage.
- **Results (n=300/matchup, two independent 150-game batches each)**: vs `dragapult_day1` (A/B) 49.3% — parity. vs `iono_day1` 63.3% vs. day1's own fresh 66.7% baseline (~3.4pt gap, <1.5 SE — treated as noise per this project's established n≥300 standard, matching two earlier "iono regression turned out to be noise" cases). vs `mega_lucario_ref` 46.0% vs day1's 48.0% (<1 SE).
- **Engineering**: zero crashes across 1000+ test games, ~0.28ms/decision avg, worst match 0.061s wall-clock — trivial overhead.
- **Why it likely underperformed the public 82%→85% result**: after removing the harmful attack-timing bonus, the plan's real influence is limited to sequencing nudges (evolve/attach/trainer order) — the actual "attack now vs. wait and build a bigger turn" decision, which is where the public technique's real value apparently comes from, was left to the base heuristic rather than genuinely being solved ahead of time by the plan.
- **Status**: **Not submitted.** Safe (no regression, negligible overhead) but statistically indistinguishable from the proven baseline. Productive next step if revisited: make the turn-plan's leaf-eval smart enough to correctly own the attack-now-vs-wait decision instead of abstaining from it.

## bc_reranker_v2 — submission crashed, root-caused, fixed, resubmitted (2026-08-12)
The first bc_reranker_v2 submission (55464774) came back `SubmissionStatus.ERROR`. Downloaded the validation episode's agent logs (`kaggle competitions episodes <id>` → `kaggle competitions logs <episode_id> <agent_index>`) and found the real cause:

```
File "/kaggle_simulations/agent/main.py", line 5, in <module>
    sys.path.insert(0, os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)))
NameError: name '__file__' is not defined
```

**Root cause**: Kaggle's sandbox loads an agent by `exec()`-ing its source directly (`kaggle_environments/agent.py`'s `get_last_callable`), not via a normal module import — so `__file__` is undefined in that context. `main.py` used `sys.path.insert(0, os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)))` to make `import features`/`import heuristic_scored` (sibling files) resolve, which crashed instantly. This bug is invisible to our local test harness because `run_match.py` loads agents via `importlib.util.spec_from_file_location`, which DOES set `__file__` correctly — so every local test (including the pre-submission tarball-extraction smoke test) passed while the exact same code was guaranteed to crash live. Grepped all agent directories: this `__file__` pattern exists only in the ML-family agents (`bc_v1`, `bc_reranker_v1`, `bc_v2`, `bc_reranker_v2`, `ml_v1`) — none of which had ever actually been submitted before, so this class of bug had never been caught. None of the proven live agents (dragapult_day1, bellibolt_v2, mega_lucario_v2, dragapult_v2/v3, dragapult_plan_v1) use this pattern.

**A second, subtler trap found while fixing**: the obvious fix (derive the directory from an already-imported package's `__file__`, e.g. `cg.__file__`, instead of main.py's own) works in the real sandbox but is ALSO unreliable in our local harness specifically, because `run_match.py` itself does a top-level `from cg.game import ...` before loading any agent — so `cg` ends up cached in `sys.modules` pointing at the harness's own copy, not the agent-under-test's, silently deriving the wrong directory. **Final fix**: stopped relying on `sys.path`/`__file__`/any cached module entirely — `main.py` now loads `features.py`/`heuristic_scored.py` directly via `importlib.util.spec_from_file_location` using the exact same bare-relative-path-with-`/kaggle_simulations/agent/`-prefix-fallback pattern already proven correct for `deck.csv`/`model.json`.
- **Verification before resubmitting**: (1) a local `exec()` simulation that reproduces the sandbox's exact failure mode (compiles and execs main.py's source into a fresh namespace with no `__file__` set) — now passes; (2) a real 20-game match via the harness using the actually-extracted, actually-packaged tarball (not source) vs `dragapult_day1` — 12-8, zero crashes, consistent with the previously-measured parity.
- **Resubmitted**: `55464921`, 2026-08-12 18:15:42, `SubmissionStatus.COMPLETE`, fresh score 600.0 (starting μ0, as expected) — confirms the crash is fixed.
- **Process note for future submissions**: local harness testing (source OR extracted-tarball) cannot catch `__file__`-dependent bugs, since `importlib`-based loading always sets `__file__` correctly regardless of what the real Kaggle sandbox does. Any future agent needing to import sibling files must use the direct-file-path-loading pattern now in `bc_reranker_v2/main.py`, never `__file__`-based `sys.path` tricks.

## dragapult_plan_v1 attack-timing fix (2026-08-12, COMPLETE — still parity, not adopted)

## bc_plan_conditioned_v1: turn-plan-context features for pure BC (2026-08-13, COMPLETE — rejected, ceiling confirmed architectural)
5th attempt at pure BC as primary policy, testing a genuinely different mechanism than the prior 4 (bc_v1, bc_v2, bc_v3_ko_override, bc_v4_ensemble, bc_dragapult_v3_combined/primary_test) — all of which varied data volume/features while keeping the model strictly pointwise (scores each decision in isolation). This attempt gives the model **turn-history context** directly: a `TurnContextTracker` (`agent/bc_plan_conditioned_v1/features.py`) accumulates, per match and reset every turn, how many decisions/energy-attaches/evolves/trainer-plays have already happened this turn, whether we've already attacked, and which Pokemon (by serial) has received the most investment this turn (the "focus" entity) — then a boolean feature flags whether the CURRENT option's target matches that focus. Causal by construction: only updated after a decision's outcome is known (offline: real chronological replay order; online: our own actual choice), never using future information — audited specifically for this per the task's leak-safety concern.

- **Data**: re-extracted the same real Dragapult-specific source games used by `bc_dragapult_v3_combined` (research/episodes/** filtered to Dragapult-ex winners, our own research/own_replays/ win-filtered games) with the 6 new features appended (138→144 dim). Got 31 public games (21,849 rows) + 59 own-ladder games (33,882 rows) = 55,731 total rows, 90 games, 8,464 decision groups — consistent with (slightly more than) the sibling investigation's 50,679-row figure.
- **Controlled ablation** (identical rows/split, only feature-dim truncated 144→138 for the control): plan-conditioned features gave a small, real, decision-level accuracy lift — **59.15% → 59.91%** (n=1,711 held-out decisions), row AUC unchanged (0.8529, as expected since AUC is per-option ranking dominated by base features; the plan features specifically help resolve which-decision-wins ties). This confirms the features aren't noise, but the effect size is modest.
- **Live-architecture test as PRIMARY policy** (same 3-layer-fallback pure-BC agent pattern as `bc_dragapult_v3_combined/primary_test`, model exported to stdlib JSON, faithfulness-verified max abs diff 1.0e-10): **6% vs dragapult_day1 (n=100), 11.7% vs iono_day1 (n=60), 26.7% vs mega_lucario_ref (n=60)**. Zero crashes. This is the same catastrophic-failure band (5-24%) as all 4 prior pure-BC attempts — the small offline accuracy gain did not translate into competitive play.

## dragapult_plan_ranked_v1: turn-level learned value model (2026-08-13, COMPLETE — safe parity, not a proven win)
Follow-up to 5 independent pure-BC-as-primary rejections (data volume, lethal features, KO-override, ensemble voting, turn-context features) — all converged on "pointwise per-option classifiers can't capture turn-spanning sequencing." This build tests the one remaining structurally-different idea: score WHOLE CANDIDATE TURNS with a learned model, integrated into `dragapult_plan_v1`'s existing search-based turn-plan architecture (which already enumerates candidate plans via rollout and picks the best by a hand-tuned formula) rather than trying to replace per-decision scoring.

- **Data**: turn-level (not decision-level) extraction from the same real Dragapult-specific replay pool (59 own live-ladder wins + 31 public top-episode-index wins, 90 games). Turn boundaries recovered from each player's own observation stream via `current.turn` value transitions (empirically verified: each distinct value covers exactly one of that player's own turns). Yielded 832 turn rows.
- **Label design (documented choice)**: win-filtered data alone can't support outcome-contrastive learning (only 2 of 60 own replays were losses — nowhere near enough for a real win/loss classifier). Used a bootstrapped 1-step state-value formulation instead: features = POST-turn state (HP/prize/bench/energy counts + actions taken, matching exactly what's available at candidate-scoring time inside `compute_turn_plan`'s rollout), label = the immediately FOLLOWING same-side turn's own prize/HP delta. - **Model**: GradientBoostingRegressor, 150 trees depth 3, GroupKFold(5) by game. MAE 2.817 vs 3.258 mean-baseline (~13.5% error reduction, real but modest signal). Exported to stdlib-walkable JSON, faithfulness verified (max abs diff 1.24e-14).
- **Integration**: additive blend into `dragapult_plan_v1`'s existing candidate-scoring loop (`compute_turn_plan`, the `score = prizes_taken*200000 - prizes_lost*150000 + ...` formula) — `score += learned_value * VALUE_BLEND_WEIGHT(4000)`. Never replaces the hand-tuned score, wrapped in try/except returning 0.0 (neutral) on any failure, same never-crash discipline as every other agent this project.
- **Results** (n=150/matchup, first checkpoint): 49.3% vs dragapult_day1, 58.7% vs iono_day1, 51.3% vs mega_lucario_ref — all comparable to `dragapult_plan_v1`'s own historical ranges, no regression.

## dragapult_wall_patch_v1: Crustle-wall anti-stall investigation — CLOSED, negative result, not shipped (2026-08-14)
Investigated the public `aristophanivan/probablity-v2` notebook's "Anti-Stall Logic" idea (shift scoring toward resource conservation when the opponent has a true-immunity wall Pokemon in play) as a candidate patch on top of `bc_reranker_v2`. Built the required test infrastructure, confirmed the gap is real, built and tested two calibrations of the patch, both came back flat-to-negative. Not submission-ready; nothing shipped.

**Test infrastructure built** (new, not touching any existing agent):
- `agent/crustle_wall_ref/` — a from-scratch, minimal rule-based pilot for `EX_06` "Mega Kangaskhan / Crustle / Petrel" (control/disruption archetype, CC BY 4.0, from `research/datasets/ptcg_22_decks/`), since no playable agent existed for this archetype anywhere in the repo. Deck (`research/reference_decks/ex06_crustle_wall_deck.csv`, already extracted) confirmed correct against `archetypes.csv`'s reported 12 Pokemon/34 Trainer/14 Energy counts. Card/attack data (HP, costs, abilities incl. Crustle's "Mysterious Rock Inn" — prevents all damage from opponent `ex` attacks) pulled directly via `cg.api.all_card_data()`/`all_attack()`. Not tuned for strength, just non-broken: zero crashes across 40 smoke-test games against all 4 of our decks (dragapult_day1, iono_day1, mega_lucario_ref, bc_reranker_v2), win rates for the wall deck in the 10-30% range against them (a real but not lockout-strength wall, as expected — only Crustle itself is immune, Kangaskhan/Ogerpon still trade damage normally).
- Instrumented diagnostic harness (scratchpad `diag_wall_gap.py`) wrapping the standard `run_match` loop to track, per game: min/final deck count reached, final turn number, Supporter/Item play counts, and probable deck-out losses (loss with final deck count ≤1).

**Step 1 — gap confirmation (bc_reranker_v2 vs crustle_wall_ref, n=250, unmodified)**: real and clearly measurable. vs the wall: 82.4% win rate, avg final turn 17.55 (max 54), 45.6% of games dipped to ≤8 cards remaining (the existing `no_draw` hard-cutoff threshold), 8.4% hit 0 remaining, 7.6% of all games ended in a probable deck-out loss. Same instrumentation vs the 3 standard references (n=200 each) for comparison: ≤8-card dips at 12.5% (dragapult_day1) / 29.5% (iono_day1) / 28.0% (mega_lucario_ref), 0-0.5% deck-outs. The wall matchup runs ~50-60% longer and decks out 15-20x more often than normal play. Gap confirmed real — proceeded to build the patch.

**Patch built**: `agent/dragapult_wall_patch_v1/` = unmodified `bc_reranker_v2`'s `main.py`/`features.py`/`model.json`/`cg`/`deck.csv` (BC reranker untouched, per constraint) + a copied, patched `heuristic_scored.py`. Added one new function, `wall_conservation_active(op_state, deck_count, hand_size)`, analogous in spirit to the existing `no_damage_dex` — reuses its exact id set (158/207/330/345) as the wall-detection signal, gated on `deck_count <= 28 and hand_size >= 3` so it only activates in the "worth conserving, not yet desperate" zone, and is a strict no-op (identical behavior to unpatched `bc_reranker_v2`) whenever no wall Pokemon is on the opponent's board. Wired into 3 targeted `hand_score()` branches: Lillie's Determination (shuffle hand + draw 6-8, the single biggest deck-cycling card in the deck), Brock's Scouting's generic branch, and Crispin's generic (non-"genuinely still need energy") branch, plus a lighter touch on the `Poke_Pad` item's PLAY score when the board is already developed.

**Two calibrations tested, both against crustle_wall_ref, n=250, isolated A/B vs unpatched bc_reranker_v2**:
- **v1 (soft floors — 5000/3000, not zero)**: had essentially no effect. Win rate 82.4% → 80.8%, ≤8-card dips 45.6% → 45.6% (unchanged), deck-outs 19 → 17 (marginal). Root cause found via direct instrumentation (`diag_card_plays.py`, tracking specific card-ID play counts): demoting Lillie/Brock just caused Crispin's un-gated generic branch to fill the same "play *a* supporter every turn" slot instead — the mechanism only lets one Supporter score >0 win the turn's `use_support` slot, so lowering some candidates just promotes the next one rather than skipping the turn's Supporter play. Total Supporter plays/game barely moved (6.30 → 6.41).
- **v2 (hard zero — Lillie/Brock/Crispin-generic-branch all forced to exactly 0 under `wall_mode`, so no positive-scoring candidate remains and the turn's Supporter play is skipped outright when nothing is genuinely needed)**: this time the intended mechanism clearly fired — Lillie's Determination usage dropped 2.66 → 1.27 plays/game (-52%), overall Supporter plays/game dropped 6.30 → 4.59 (-27%), and avg min/final deck count *did* improve (11.34 → 13.04 avg min, more cards preserved). But **win rate dropped further** (82.4% → 78.4%) and **deck-out losses got worse, not better** (19/250 → 23/250), and games got longer on average (17.55 → 19.06 turns). Reading: the suppressed draws were providing real tactical value (finding better lines, key pieces, useful Trainers) that outweighed the raw card-count savings — less card selection meant longer, worse-fought games, which then decked out at least as often despite drawing fewer cards per game. A textbook instance of this project's own repeatedly-documented pattern: a locally-reasoned conservation heuristic that looks correct in isolation underperforms once it actually changes play, because it can't distinguish "unnecessary" draws from ones that are tactically load-bearing.
- **3-standard-reference regression check** (v2, n=200 each): dragapult_day1 45.5% (baseline ~47.0%), iono_day1 64.5% (baseline ~66.0%), mega_lucario_ref 49.0% (baseline ~46.0%) — all within normal n=200 run-to-run noise (~3.5pp), confirming `wall_mode` correctly never fires against non-wall decks and there is no regression on the 3 standard references. This part of the test came back clean.

**Verdict**: gap is real, patch is correctly isolated and doesn't touch the BC layer or regress the 3 standard references, but **it does not improve the matchup it targets — it measurably makes the target matchup worse under both calibrations tried**. Per the task's own stop condition, **not packaged as a submission candidate**; no `submission.tar.gz` built for `dragapult_wall_patch_v1`. `agent/crustle_wall_ref/` (the test opponent) and `agent/dragapult_wall_patch_v1/` (both patch calibrations' final state = v2, the harder one) are left in place as a documented negative result / reusable test fixture, not as something ready to ship. If revisited, the more promising angle per this investigation's own evidence is probably NOT "suppress draw supporters" but something that shortens the game itself (e.g. explicitly prioritizing non-walled prize targets or forcing Crustle off the field via Boss's Orders-style switching) — conserving cards in a fight we're still going to grind through for 19+ turns doesn't help if the grinding itself is the problem.

**Correction to the above entry**: the 82.4% baseline win rate `bc_reranker_v2` already gets vs `crustle_wall_ref` (unpatched) is GOOD, not a problem to fix — it was mischaracterized in earlier reporting. ## dragapult_wall_gust_v1: verifying (not assuming) the existing Boss's-Orders-redirect mechanism — CLOSED, verified-neutral, not shipped (2026-08-14)
Follow-up per direct instruction not to preemptively write off the "gust a non-immune target into active via Boss's Orders, attack that instead of the walled Crustle" mechanism that a code read suggested already exists in `bc_reranker_v2`'s `main_option_proc()` (the `for i, pokemon in enumerate(cards)` loop already iterates opponent active+bench as attack targets, already zeroes Crustle's value via `no_damage_dex` regardless of board position, and already scores `Boss_Orders` at 60000 whenever the best plan points at a bench index). Rather than trust that read, built real instrumentation and measured it directly, reusing `crustle_wall_ref` and the existing diagnostic patterns (new script, `diag_gust_mechanism2.py`, hooking `bc_reranker_v2`'s live `plan_a` global + tracking `op.prize` across turn boundaries — an earlier version of this script had a same-turn-only KO-detection bug, fixed once a same-turn KO landed on our LAST action and wasn't observable until our next turn's first callback).

**Step 1 — does the mechanism fire, and does it work? (bc_reranker_v2 vs crustle_wall_ref, n=250, unpatched)**: Yes, both. Across 2,751 of our turns, Crustle was the opponent's active Pokemon at turn-start 44.8% of the time; of those, `plan_a` pointed at a bench target 42.0% of the time (517 turns). Boss's Orders was actually played in only 45.6% of those (236/517) — but when it WAS played, it converted to a same-turn-or-next-turn KO 31.8% of the time (75/236), 68 of which were outright game-ending turns. When Boss's Orders was NOT played in a bench-plan turn, KO rate was 0.7% (2/281) — confirming the redirect is genuinely load-bearing when it happens. Breaking down the 281 "not played" turns: 72.2% simply didn't have Boss's Orders in hand (nothing to fix, pure card availability — only 3 copies in 60 cards); the other 27.8% (78 turns) DID have it in hand but the turn's one-Supporter-per-turn slot was **already spent on something else** — and in 100% of those cases (78/78, zero unexplained misses), confirming the scoring logic itself is correct and the only failure mode is same-turn sequencing.

**Root cause found**: `main_option_proc()` only computes `plan_a` (and therefore only lets Boss's Orders score its 60000) when `can_main_attack` is ALREADY true that decision. Early in a turn, before this turn's own energy attach happens, that's false even when it's about to become true by turn's end — so on an earlier decision this same turn, Crispin's "I still need energy" branch (also gated on `not can_main_attack`, scoring 55000) wins the Supporter slot instead, and by the time energy lands and the attack becomes real, the slot is already spent.

**Fix built** (`agent/dragapult_wall_gust_v1/`, fresh copy of `bc_reranker_v2`, BC layer untouched): added `dragapult_one_attach_from_attack()` — a narrow, verifiable check (active Dragapult ex has exactly 1 energy, this turn's energy-attach hasn't been used yet, hand holds the specific basic energy type still needed) that lets `main_option_proc` compute the plan one decision earlier, ONLY in the case where the attack is guaranteed reachable this same turn (not a general relaxation). Confirmed via a direct call-counter that the gate fires as designed: 60 true / 1,806 decisions across 60 games (~1/game — narrow, as expected from the condition).

**A/B results (n=250 per matchup unless noted)**:
- vs crustle_wall_ref: win rate **exactly tied, 200/250 (80.0%) both patched and unpatched**. The targeted mechanism DID measurably improve in isolation — Boss's-Orders-play-rate-when-planned rose 45.6%→49.6%, and successful redirect-KOs/game rose from 0.30 to 0.376 (+25% relative in that specific pathway, 75→94 conversions/250 games) — but this did not translate into a detectable aggregate win-rate gain at this sample size, most likely because the affected turns (~1/game) are a small enough slice that the extra KOs mostly accelerate wins already in hand rather than flip losses into wins.
- 3-standard-reference regression check: `dragapult_day1` tied exactly (117/250 both patched and baseline, 46.8%). `mega_lucario_ref` 123/250 (49.2%) vs baseline 130/250 (52.0%), a 2.8pp gap, within 1 SE (~3.2pp) of noise. `iono_day1` showed high intrinsic run-to-run variance discovered while sanity-checking this result — baseline alone ranged 139/250 (55.6%) to 160/250 (64.0%) across two independent re-runs of the SAME unpatched agent, and patched ranged 153/250 (61.2%) to 177/250 (70.8%) across two runs; patched trended a few points above baseline on average but the spread is too wide relative to the gap to call it a real difference at this N. No run showed a clear regression in either direction.
- Zero crashes across all runs (smoke test + full instrumentation + regression suite, several thousand games total this investigation).

**Verdict**: the mechanism was NOT silently broken — it already works and converts well when it fires, exactly as the code read suggested it might. A real, narrow sequencing gap was found and verifiably fixed (confirmed via direct instrumentation, not just code inspection), but the fix's effect on OVERALL win rate is not statistically distinguishable from zero at n=250, and no regression was found on the 3 standard references. This is a genuine, evidenced neutral result, not a repeat of "seems reasonable, didn't check" — **not packaged as a submission candidate** (no `agent/dragapult_wall_gust_v1/submission.tar.gz` built) since there's no measurable win-rate case for spending a submission slot on it, but the code is low-risk, mechanistically verified, and left in place in case a larger-N test later shows the small positive trend is real.

Mechanically confirmed via `EN Card Data.csv`: Mega Brave (270) + Premium Power Pro (+30, an Item, "before Weakness/Resistance") = exactly 300 damage, comfortably >= Dragapult ex's 320 max HP, and Dragapult ex has no listed weakness to Fighting so there's no defensive lever there. Root cause in the code: `do_switch`'s only gate is `not can_main_attack` (`heuristic_scored.py`) -- once Dragapult ex can attack at all, RETREAT is unconditionally scored -1, with zero consideration of whether that turn's attack has any payoff or whether the position is fatal. (Separately noted, not pursued: 66/129 of these KOs happened to a benched Pokemon that was never even our active at time of death -- almost certainly `mega_lucario_ref`'s 2x Boss's Orders gusting a benched ex into the kill zone; flagged as a candidate for future investigation, out of scope here.)

**Fix built and tested on a fresh copy** (`agent/dragapult_combined_final_v3_lucario/`, copied from `dragapult_combined_final_v2`, single-file change to `heuristic_scored.py`): ported `_op_best_attack_damage`/`_active_in_lethal_danger` unchanged from `dragapult_patched_v1`'s already-validated Patch D (analytic, weakness-aware, no search), plus a new `plan_a_lethal` flag (True iff `main_option_proc`'s finally-chosen plan hits its own 50000 "achieves the needed prize count" sentinel). Added a narrow new retreat trigger, `no_trade_danger_retreat`: fires only when `can_main_attack` is True AND `plan_a_lethal` is False (this turn's attack secures no KO) AND our active is genuinely in the opponent's next-turn lethal range AND a bench Pokemon exists to retreat to. Deliberately additive (OR'd into the existing `do_switch`), never touches the lethal-attack case or the existing bench_attacker/Budew retreat paths. Gated behind `PATCH_ABLATE` env var (`"L"` disables) for clean isolated A/B.

**Isolated ablation, same file/process, patch ON vs patch OFF, both vs `mega_lucario_ref`, n=250 each**: **PATCH-ON 35.6% (89/250) vs PATCH-OFF 48.4% (121/250)** -- a large, clean, ~13pt regression (SE~3.2pp at n=250, so >4 SE, unambiguous, not noise). The fix makes the matchup measurably worse, not better.

**Why, most likely** (partial mechanism check, `diag_retreat_mechanism.py`, n=150): the new retreat fired 0.83x/game; of those firings, the opponent played Boss's Orders somewhere later in the same game 54% of the time and the retreated Pokemon was eventually KO'd anyway in 59% of cases -- consistent with, though not airtight proof of, the likely explanation: retreating forfeits a turn of Phantom Dive's actual win condition (the 60 bench-counter damage this deck relies on to set up *future* multi-KO turns, not just this-turn trades) while `mega_lucario_ref`'s 2x Boss's Orders can simply re-force the retreated Pokemon back into the active slot next turn anyway, negating the positional benefit entirely. This is the same shape of failure this project has now seen twice (`dragapult_wall_patch_v1`'s conservation-heuristic finding): a locally-reasoned defensive heuristic that looks obviously correct in isolation backfires once it's actually measured, because it can't see the opponent's own countertool.
